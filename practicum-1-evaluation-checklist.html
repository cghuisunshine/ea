Practicum 1 – Evaluation Checklist

The Assessment Checklist is intended to guide both the School Supervisor and EA Host in providing ongoing feedback to the EA Practicum Student throughout the practicum. Two formal student evaluations are to be completed by the School Supervisor, in collaboration with the EA Host in advance of the meeting with the Practicum Supervisor. The midterm evaluation should be complete by the midpoint of the practicum, to provide the EA Practicum Students with the opportunity to adjust their ongoing performance for the remainder of their practicum experience.

Due to placement limitations, it is acknowledged that EA Practicum Students may not have the opportunity to engage with or demonstrate ALL the criteria listed. However, School Supervisors are encouraged to make every effort to provide each EA Practicum Student with access to as many learning opportunities as possible.

The final evaluation will be held near the end of the practicum, with the expectation that the EA Practicum Student will have met or exceeded all the Assessment Checklist criteria. Please ensure all team members sign the last page of this document.

Accountability and Safety

  1. Arrives prepared and on time.
  2. Uses personal electronic devices only during designated breaks times*.
  3. Able to explain the legal responsibility to report incidents of child abuse or neglect.
  4. Uses feedback to enhance skills and abilities.
  5. Behaves in a professional manner at all times.

* unless it’s required by the EA Host.

Professional Attire

  1. Wears comfortable, professional clothing that allows for physical activity, with full coverage, and without questionable logos or references, rips, or tears.
  2. Wears supportive footwear with non-slip soles, securely attached to the foot.
  3. Secures long hair and removes loose items, such as long jewelry.

Ethics and Respect

  1. Maintains professional boundaries with school staff, children/youth, and families.
  2. Protects confidential child/youth, staff, and parent information, verbally and in writing.
  3. Discussions about children/youth are conducted in a way to ensure their privacy and dignity at all times.
  4. Accepts and supports the decisions and directions of school staff.
  5. Demonstrates a strong awareness and practice of respectful language related to race, culture, religion, sexual orientation, gender identity, and disability.

Communication and Collaboration

  1. Models respectful and effective verbal and non-verbal communication.
  2. Respects other’s ideas and perspectives.
  3. Communicates child/youth progress to the classroom teacher, verbally and in writing.
  4. Reviews all written information, as available, and asks questions to gather more information helpful to the support of the children youth.
  5. Demonstrates an understanding that home/school communications are to be conducted by the classroom teacher, and directs parent inquiries to the teacher.
  6. Demonstrates a strong ability to communicate effectively in English.
  7. Engages with students and school staff in a positive and cheerful manner.

Administrative Tasks / Non-Instructional Support

  1. Knows where all children/youth documentation is located. (e.g. IEP, SRP, PBS Plan)
  2. With support, records and tracks data as outlined in student IEPs.
  3. Understands roles and responsibilities of school and district staff.

Student Support

  1. Demonstrates an understanding of inclusion, and the ability to facilitate it.
  2. Takes initiative to support children/youth needs, while taking direction from the classroom teacher and EA Host.
  3. Demonstrates an ability to establish themselves as an authority figure in the class.
  4. Provides specific, positive feedback to children/youth, with a focus on competencies, to build confidence and self esteem.
  5. Provides clear and consistent direction, and follows through.
  6. With assistance, uses supports to support inclusion and self regulation. (visual schedule, timers, transition countdown, contingency maps, social stories)
  7. With assistance, reinforces socially appropriate behaviour.
  8. With assistance, understands and implements break and reward times.
  9. With assistance, collects and reviews data to assess social and behavioural needs.
  10. With assistance, seeks to understand the function of student behaviour.
  11. With assistance, encourages child/youth engagement amongst peers during instructional and non-instructional time
  12. With assistance, facilitates opportunities to develop friendships by creating or developing connections amongst children/youth.
  13. Understands the impact of different experiences and cultural backgrounds.

Facilitating Independence

  1. With assistance, uses developmentally and age appropriate adaptations and modifications to support chil/youth inclusion in the classroom
  2. With assistance, uses the lowest level of prompting required, as outlined in the Prompting Hierarchy
  3. With assistance, identifies strategies that may be used to reduce student reliance on EA support
  4. With assistance, provides opportunities for child/youth to practice self-monitoring and problem solving
  5. Honours child/youth perspective and input
