Scenario B: Becky

Grade 4 student diagnosed with Autism — IEP focus: increasing social communication skills

IEP Goal: Becky will appropriately initiate and respond to peers during structured activities in 4 out of 5 opportunities, with no more than one prompt.
Need Support / Strategy Concrete Example
Objective Build appropriate initiation and response skills with peers Becky will initiate or respond appropriately in 4 out of 5 opportunities, with no more than one prompt
Visual support Use visual conversation cues and sentence starters “Can I play?” / “What do you think?” / “My turn, your turn.”
Modelling Teacher or EA models the expected social interaction Show Becky how to greet, ask a question, or join a group
Role-play Practice common social situations in a safe setting Practice joining a game, asking for help, or taking turns
Peer practice Provide structured opportunities with supportive peers Use partner work, small-group games, or recess practice
Reinforcement Give immediate, specific positive feedback “Great job asking your classmate a question.”
Evaluation Track performance using a simple checklist Record: Independent / Prompted / Not completed; review progress weekly

Simple Teaching Approach

Teach Model Practice Reinforce Fade Support
Example: Before a partner activity, teach Becky one specific skill: asking a peer a relevant question. Model the skill, let her practise with a peer, provide brief positive feedback, and gradually reduce prompting.

Presentation Summary

Objective → Initiate/respond to peers in 4/5 opportunities
Strategy → Visual cues + modelling + role-play + peer practice
Evaluation → Daily observation/checklist + weekly review
Key Point: Make social communication observable and measurable: Objective → Strategy → Evaluation. Teach one skill at a time, practise it naturally, and fade prompts as Becky becomes more independent.
