Nonverbal communication · Unit 1

Twenty-four faces, one sweater

Every frame on the slide is the same person in the same grey top, so the only thing that changes is the body. Below, each one is broken into the cue — what the hands, head and eyes are actually doing — and what it usually signals. Seven of them carry a warning, because the cue on its own points two ways at once.

01

At ease

Open & warm

CueShoulders down, arms hanging loose, a small closed-mouth smile, gaze level.

Reads asRelaxed and available. Nothing is being managed or performed.

02

Hands in the hair

Overwhelmed

CueBoth hands pushed up into her hair, elbows flared wide, eyebrows lifted, mouth slack.

Reads asOverload. Something has just landed and she has nowhere to put it yet.

Read with careHands-to-head reads as panic in adults but often as shame in children. Check what happened ten seconds earlier.

03

Leaning in

Open & warm

CueTorso angled toward you, head tilted, one hand drawn to her chest, open smile.

Reads asInterest and warmth. The lean is the tell — people close distance to what they like.

04

Off somewhere else

Thinking it over

CueCheek propped on the heel of her hand, eyes lifted and away, mouth soft.

Reads asDrifting — remembering something, or elsewhere entirely.

Read with careEasily misread as defiance. Eyes up and soft is daydreaming; eyes up and hard is an eye-roll.

05

Caught off guard

Alert & surprised

CuePalms up beside an open mouth, fingers spread, eyes wide, chin pulled back.

Reads asAlarm — surprise arriving before she has decided whether it is good news or bad.

Read with careThe same shape as delighted shock. Only the eyes and what follows tell you which.

06

Blocking it out

Overwhelmed

CueOne hand clamped over temple and eye, head turned away, brow pinched.

Reads asHad enough. Self-soothing with one hand while shutting off the view with the same gesture.

07

Straining to recall

Thinking it over

CueFingertips pressed to both temples, eyes rolled up, mouth slightly open.

Reads asEffortful concentration — searching for something, not distressed by it.

Read with careOne notch away from a headache. Tension in the jaw separates the two.

08

Thrilled

Open & warm

CueBoth hands up beside her face, palms out, mouth wide, eyes creased at the corners.

Reads asDelight, and genuine — the eyes are doing as much work as the mouth.

Read with careHands and mouth are identical to a scream of fear. Crinkled eyes are the difference.

09

Neutral

Baseline

CueSquared up, arms at her sides, face at rest, steady gaze.

Reads asNo signal being sent. Composed and waiting.

Read with careUse this frame as the control. Every other reading here is a departure from it.

10

Disbelief

Overwhelmed

CueBoth hands flat on top of her head, elbows out, jaw dropped.

Reads asThis cannot be happening. The hands pin the head in place while the face catches up.

11

Content

Open & warm

CuePalms pressed together against one cheek, eyes closed, small smile, head tipped.

Reads asAffection or mock-sleep. A warm, deliberately soft signal — usually playful.

12

Not convinced

Thinking it over

CueHand resting along her jaw, one eyebrow raised, eyes cut sideways.

Reads asWeighing it up. Sceptical, still deciding, not yet arguing.

13

Arms folded

Closed off

CueArms crossed low, shoulders squared, brow drawn down, direct stare.

Reads asClosed off and holding a position.

Read with careFolded arms alone mean very little — people are cold, or just comfortable. The furrowed brow is what makes this one resistance.

14

Deflated

Closed off

CueHead dropped onto her hand, shoulders collapsed, eyes down and unfocused.

Reads asThe energy has left the body. Bored, tired, or flat.

Read with careThe most commonly misread frame in a classroom. Flatness is as often low mood as disinterest.

15

Say that again

Alert & surprised

CueChin pushed forward, mouth open, eyes wide and fixed on you.

Reads asStartled — and the forward lean turns it into a challenge rather than a retreat.

16

Laughing

Open & warm

CueHead thrown back, mouth open, eyes squeezed nearly shut, hand at her cheek.

Reads asReal laughter. The head travelling backwards and away is the giveaway.

Read with careHead back with the eyes open and the brow raised is a groan, not a laugh.

17

Propped up

Closed off

CueBoth hands cupping her jaw, elbows planted, eyelids heavy.

Reads asGlum and holding herself upright. Sadness or plain exhaustion.

18

The shrug

Closed off

CueBoth palms turned up, elbows in, shoulders raised, mouth open in a frown.

Reads asExasperation. Palms up is an appeal; the frown turns it into a protest.

19

Shutting it out

Overwhelmed

CueBoth hands over her ears, mouth wide open, eyes shut tight.

Reads asSensory or emotional overload, with input being actively cut off.

Read with careTreat this as a request rather than defiance. Lower the noise before asking for anything.

20

Dismay

Alert & surprised

CueFingers spread over both cheeks, eyes wide, mouth small and open.

Reads asShe has seen something she does not want to be true.

21

Quiet, please

Deliberate signal

CueIndex finger vertical against her lips, eyes on you, everything else still.

Reads asAn instruction, given on purpose. One of the few frames here that is communication rather than leaked feeling.

22

Open delight

Open & warm

CueHead turned over her shoulder, mouth wide, eyes bright and creased.

Reads asCaught mid-laugh and aimed at someone specific.

23

At the limit

Overwhelmed

CueBoth hands dragged back through her hair, hairline pushed up, brows pulled together.

Reads asFrustration with nowhere to go. The grooming gesture is stress leaking out through the hands.

24

Coy

Open & warm

CueHead tilted onto her shoulder, hand near her collar, small closed smile.

Reads asPleased and slightly shy. The tilt exposes the neck — a low-threat, friendly signal.

Cues cluster, they don't stand alone

Folded arms mean almost nothing by themselves; folded arms with a lowered brow and a hard stare mean something specific. Look for three signals agreeing before you commit to a reading.

The baseline is the reading

Plate 09 is the control frame. What matters with a student is not whether they look withdrawn today, but whether they look withdrawn compared with how they usually sit, stand and speak.

Check before you conclude

The safest move with any of these is to name what you see and ask, rather than announce what you think it means: "You've gone quiet — is this a bit much right now?"
