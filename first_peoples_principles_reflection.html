First Peoples Principles of Learning and Education

I’d choose Topic 2: First Peoples Principles of Learning and Education because it connects naturally to your EA training and gives you room for personal reflection.

Before today’s class

Before today’s class, my understanding of education was mainly focused on helping students learn academic knowledge and skills. I thought a good education meant that teachers explained things clearly and students worked hard to understand and practise them. Today, I learned that the First Peoples Principles of Learning have a broader view of education. Learning is not only about academic achievement. It also involves relationships, culture, community, identity, and personal well-being.

What stood out to me

One idea that stood out to me is that learning takes time and is different for every student. Students may have different backgrounds, experiences, strengths, and needs. As a future Education Assistant, I should be patient and respectful instead of expecting every student to learn in the same way or at the same speed. I also learned that building a trusting relationship with students can be an important part of supporting their learning.

My main takeaway

My main takeaway is that education should support the whole person, not only academic performance. In my future work as an EA, I want to listen carefully, respect each student’s identity and experiences, and help students feel safe and included at school. I believe this approach can help students become more confident and independent learners.
