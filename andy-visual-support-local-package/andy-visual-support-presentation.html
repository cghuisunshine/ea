Visual Support for Andy – “It’s Too Loud!”

A presentation script about helping Andy identify his feelings, communicate what he needs, and choose a safe support option.

Interactive board How Andy uses it Why it helps Implementation Goal

How Andy uses it

When Andy is upset or overwhelmed, he can first point to how he is feeling. Then, instead of leaving the classroom without asking, he can choose one of the support options.

1. Choose a feeling

  • Bored
  • Overwhelmed
  • Nervous
  • Mad
  • Sad

2. Choose what would help

  • Take a break
  • Go for a walk
  • Have a snack
  • Use headphones
  • Read a superhero book
  • Ask for support
  • Use the bathroom
  • Go to the sensory room

The large box in the middle of the visual can show the feeling and the option Andy chooses. For example:

“I feel nervous → I need headphones.”

“I feel mad → I need a break.”

Helpful addition Add one clear, easy-to-find button that says “I need a break” or “Too loud.” This gives Andy a quick way to communicate when choosing from several options feels difficult.

Why it will help Andy

Andy uses an iPad to communicate, becomes nervous when many people are around him, and may leave the room when it becomes too loud. This visual gives him an easy way to communicate before he becomes overwhelmed.

It also includes things Andy already likes. Cucumbers and cheese can be represented in the snack option, and superheroes are included as a calming activity. These familiar choices make the visual more personal and meaningful to him.

How we would implement it

We would teach Andy how to use the board while he is calm, rather than introducing it only when he is already upset.

  1. An adult models the first question: “Andy, how are you feeling? Choose one.”
  2. The adult asks: “What would help you?”
  3. Andy chooses a feeling and then selects a support option.
  4. The adult acknowledges his choice and helps him access the appropriate support.
  5. We practise the routine regularly so Andy can use it more independently over time.

Goal

The goal is for Andy to recognize when he is becoming overwhelmed and use communication before leaving the room.

“I’m becoming overwhelmed → I communicate how I feel → I choose what I need → I stay safe.”

This approach respects Andy’s need for relief from noise while teaching a safe, clear, and socially appropriate way to ask for help.
