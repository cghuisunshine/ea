Underlying Needs in Conflict

Two simple examples: cooking dinner and cleaning after work.

The goal is to understand what each person really needs, not just what they say they want.

Example 1: Who Cooks Dinner?

Conflict: A husband and wife disagree about who should cook dinner.

Wife: “It’s your turn to cook. I cooked yesterday.”
Husband: “I’m tired. I don’t want to cook tonight.”

Underlying Needs

Wife:
Autonomy, fairness, and support
Husband:
Rest and relaxation
Possible solutions:
  • Order food
  • Cook together
  • Make something simple
  • Go to a restaurant

Example 2: Cleaning After Work — Safety

Conflict: The couple needs to do some cleaning outside after work, but it is already dark.

Person A: “We need to finish the cleaning outside tonight.”
Person B: “I’m scared to go outside in the dark. I want to stay inside.”

Underlying Needs

Person A:
Responsibility and accomplishment
Person B:
Safety — physical and emotional
Possible solution:

They can do simple indoor cleaning tonight and leave the outdoor cleaning until daylight tomorrow.

Key Message

A position is what a person says they want.

An underlying need explains why that choice is important to them.

When people understand each other’s needs, they can find a solution more easily.

Short Presentation Script

“The second example is about safety. One person wants to finish the cleaning, but the other person feels unsafe going outside in the dark. Instead of arguing, they can understand the underlying need for safety and do the outdoor work the next day.”
