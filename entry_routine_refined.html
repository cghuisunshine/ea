Entry Routine

Student Independence Support Plan
Goal: The student will enter the school, move to the classroom, put away belongings, and get ready for class with gradually reduced adult support.

Entry Routine Steps

1. Bell or arrival cue occurs.
2. Enter the school.
3. Walk to the classroom.
4. Go to the assigned place.
5. Take off coat and backpack.
6. Put belongings away.
7. Get materials ready.
8. Sit down and prepare to learn.

Prompting Hierarchy — Entry Examples

Prompt Level Example for Entry
Natural Cue The bell rings. The student independently enters the school and goes to the classroom.
Gesture The EA points toward the entrance or classroom door.
Visual / Picture The EA shows a picture, transition card, or visual checklist showing “Go inside → Classroom → Backpack away → Sit down.”
Verbal The EA says, “It’s time to go inside,” or asks, “What is next?”
Model The EA or a peer demonstrates how to enter, walk to class, and put belongings away. A short video model can also be used.
Partial Physical The EA gives a light touch at the elbow or shoulder to guide the student toward the entrance.
Full Physical The EA provides full physical guidance or support when needed to complete the entry step safely.

Additional Supports for Entry

Countdown “5 minutes until we go inside… 2 minutes… 1 minute.” Helps the student prepare for the transition.
First / Then / Next “First go inside, then put your backpack away, next sit at your desk.”
Social Story Read a short story about what happens when arriving at school and what the student is expected to do.
Modelling — Peer or Video Show the student a peer completing the entry routine, or use a short video model.
Transition Card Give the student a card with a picture or words such as “Go to Classroom” to support the move inside.
Calm-Down Routine If entry is stressful, use a brief routine such as stop, breathe, count, and then enter.
Visual Schedule Show where “Entry” fits in the student’s morning schedule.
Visual Checklist Example: □ Go inside   □ Classroom   □ Backpack away   □ Sit down
Token Board / Rewards Give a token or praise after completing agreed entry steps. Gradually fade the reward as independence increases.
Forward Chaining Teach the first step independently first, then gradually add the next steps.
Backward Chaining Adult supports most of the routine, while the student completes the final step independently first. Gradually add earlier steps.

How to Fade Support

Full Physical → Partial Physical → Model → Verbal → Visual/Picture → Gesture → Natural Cue

Use only as much support as the student needs. Gradually reduce prompts so the natural school cues become enough for the student to complete the entry routine independently.
