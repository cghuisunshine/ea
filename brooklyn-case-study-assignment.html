Brooklyn Case StudyA positive behaviour support plan across three behaviour pathways

Names  Peter, Sue, Sarah Date  September 6, 2026 Marks  30 total
01

Background Information

/ 1

Brooklyn is 16 and lives with foster parents and their children, who include him in family and community activities. Monthly visits with his biological mother are difficult for him and may be followed by more intense behaviour at home and a more difficult week at school. Both his foster family and school team are supportive and want what is best for Brooklyn.

02

School Information

/ 3

Preferred activities

Brooklyn enjoys community outings, his weekly recycling job and the Slurpee that follows, cooking and laundry activities, business cards and brochures, and carrying his daily planner to classes.

Strengths

Brooklyn is successful with repetitive tasks such as folding laundry, stocking shelves, wiping tables, and sorting recycling. He takes responsibility for the weekly recycling job, participates in community activities, and is learning to use a daily planner to organize his school day.

Areas of difficulty

Brooklyn has difficulty regulating strong emotions following stressful family contact. At school, he has difficulty accepting limits around business cards, interacting with Teddy during reading, and completing handwriting tasks because of poor fine-motor skills. He may avoid journal writing by staying in the washroom for up to 25 minutes.

  • Strong emotions after visits with his biological mother (self-regulation).
  • Taking all the business cards when the display is full, and sometimes becoming angry when escorted out (self-regulation; communication).
  • Appearing anxious around Teddy and yelling when Teddy asks what is wrong (self-regulation; social skills and communication).
  • Holding a pen or pencil because of poor fine motor skills. When asked to write, he may spend 25 minutes or more in the washroom and miss much of journal time (fine motor skills; task management).
03

Behaviour Pathways

/ 12
Pathway 1

Business cards

Setting event
  • Brooklyn is on a community walk and visits the town’s visitor centre.
  • He has a strong interest in business cards and brochures. He spends a long time looking at and reading them and likes the idea of a job where he can hand out a card.
  • A difficult week after a visit with his biological mother.
Antecedent

Brooklyn enters the visitor centre and sees a full display of business cards.

Behaviour

Brooklyn takes many or all of the business cards and puts them in his pockets.

Consequence

The EA escorts Brooklyn out of the visitor centre. This can sometimes make him very angry.

Possible function

Seeking all business cards.

Lagging skills
social skills communication self-regulation
Desired behaviour

By June 2027, during visits to the visitor centre, Brooklyn will independently ask permission before taking business cards, take only the agreed-upon number of cards, and leave the visitor centre calmly with the group without requiring an individual EA escort, in 4 out of 5 consecutive visits.

Pathway 2

Reading group with Teddy

Setting event
  • Brooklyn and Teddy are placed in the same reading group. Brooklyn appears anxious when they are together.
  • Monthly visit with his biological mother.
Antecedent

Teddy asks Brooklyn what is wrong during reading time.

Behaviour

Brooklyn yells at Teddy at a very high volume.

Consequence

The EA or teacher separates Brooklyn and Teddy, breaks up the group, and reads to them separately.

Possible function

Avoiding Teddy and seeking EA attention.

Lagging skills
social skills self-regulation communication executive function
Alternative behaviour

By February 2027, when Brooklyn feels uncomfortable or overwhelmed during a reading group with Teddy, he will calmly say, “I need space,” show a break/space card, or ask an adult for help instead of yelling, in 4 out of 5 opportunities.

Pathway 3

Journal writing

Setting event
  • It is hard for Brooklyn to hold the pencil — poor fine motor skills.
  • Monthly visit with his biological mother.
Antecedent

Brooklyn is asked to write his journal daily.

Behaviour

Brooklyn asks to use the washroom and may stay there for up to 25 minutes.

Consequence

The EA knocks and tells him to hurry, and Brooklyn misses a large portion of the journal block.

Possible function

Avoiding journal writing and?

Lagging skills
fine motor skills, especially holding a pen or pencil self-regulation communication executive function
Alternative behaviour

By February 2027, when journal writing feels difficult, Brooklyn will appropriately ask for help, request a brief break, or request an adapted writing option such as typing instead of avoiding the task by remaining in the washroom, in 4 out of 5 opportunities.

  1. I like going to the visitor centre with my school group and looking at the business cards and brochures.
  2. Sometimes the display is full of cards that catch my attention.
  3. I may want to take all of the cards because business cards are very interesting to me.
  4. I can feel excited when I see cards I really like.
  5. Feeling excited is okay. People get excited about things they enjoy.
  6. If I take all of the cards without asking, there will be no cards left for other visitors.
  7. The staff, other visitors, or my EA may feel concerned when that happens.
  8. They may ask me to return the cards and remind me of the visitor-centre rules.
  9. If I get upset, my EA may escort me out of the visitor centre, and I may feel angry or frustrated.
  10. When I see cards I want, I can pause before I touch them.
  11. I can take one slow breath and keep my hands calm while I decide what to do.
  12. Then I can ask first, wait for the answer, take only the number I am allowed to take, and leave with my group when it is time to go.
  13. When I do that, the staff, visitors, and my EA can feel comfortable and respected.
  14. They can respond positively because I am following the rules and showing that I can handle the visit.
  15. I get to keep the cards I was allowed to take and stay with my group. I can feel proud of doing it on my own.
  16. I can enjoy collecting business cards and still make choices that work well for me and for other people.

  1. I sometimes work in the same reading group as Teddy.
  2. During reading, Teddy may ask me a question such as, “What’s wrong?”

  4. I might feel tense, nervous, or upset.

  13. When I communicate this way, Teddy and the adults can understand what I need.
  14. They can give me space, a short break, or help me with the situation.
  15. I can get the support I need without yelling. I can feel safer, calmer, and more in control.
  16. I do not have to answer a personal question. I can communicate my need for space in a respectful way.

  1. I have journal-writing time at school.
  2. Sometimes writing by hand is hard for me because holding a pencil can be difficult.

  4. I can feel frustrated, tired, or stressed.

  13. When I tell an adult what I need, my teacher or EA can understand me better.
  14. They can give me appropriate help or a short break.
  15. I can get support and then return to the activity. That can help me feel calmer and more successful.
  16. Asking for help or a break is a strong way to handle a difficult task.
