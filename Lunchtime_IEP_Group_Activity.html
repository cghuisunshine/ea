Lunchtime Activity

Planning an inclusive outside free-time activity based on a student’s IEP goal, interests, strengths, and learning preferences.

Planning focus

Choose an IEP goal that can be practised during outside free time. Consider the student’s interests, learning preferences, strengths, stretches, overall goals, and big ideas.

Lunchtime activity plan

Group members Sue · Emily · Peter · Mary
Student with an IEP Matt
Goal chosen With support, I can be part of a group.
Activity Cooperative train-track building: In a small group, Matt and his peers will use straws as track pieces and small plastic containers as stations. They will take turns choosing, placing, and sharing materials to complete one train route together.
Materials needed Straws, small plastic containers, tape, bottle tops, a visual step card, turn-taking cards, and a two-minute timer.
Including other students Frontload the activity with a short visual demonstration. Invite peers who are interested in trains, give each child a simple role—track builder, station builder, or train driver—and rotate the roles so everyone contributes and practises taking turns.
Student strengths used Matt can identify people and things that are important to him and has a strong sense of his likes and dislikes. He enjoys trains and playing with friends, learns by watching peers, and can follow routines when the steps are visual and manageable.
How the activity matches the goal The shared construction task gives Matt repeated opportunities to join a group, make a choice, ask for materials, wait, and give a peer a turn. The train theme uses his interest, while the visual model and rotating roles make the expectations predictable.
