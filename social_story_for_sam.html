Social Story for Sam

My Morning Classroom Routine

A clear routine to help me feel safe, ready, and successful at school.

a) Social Story for Sam

When I come to school in the morning, I may want to go to the centres. Centres are fun, but they are not open yet.

First, I enter the classroom and look at the morning schedule.

I go to my place at the whiteboard. The teacher may give me a special job, such as changing the day’s visual or turning the class light on.

I keep the teacher’s magnets and the light switch safe. I use classroom materials only when the teacher says they are ready.

If waiting feels difficult, I can:

  • Look at my visual schedule.
  • Ask, “When are centres?”
  • Choose a waiting activity.
  • Ask for a short break.
  • Use a calming tool.

When the morning activity is finished, I check my schedule again. The schedule will show me when it is time for centres.

Following the morning routine helps everyone feel safe and ready to learn. I can be proud of myself for completing my morning routine.

b) Strategies to Accompany the Social Story

  • Visual schedule: Show a clear sequence such as:
    Enter classroom → Morning job → Whiteboard activity → Centres
  • First–Then board: “First whiteboard, then centres.”
  • Choice of classroom jobs: Let Sam choose between two appropriate jobs, such as changing the date or handing out materials.
  • Replacement activities: Provide safe magnets, a mini whiteboard, a sensory item, or another hands-on waiting activity.
  • Functional communication: Teach Sam to say or show cards for “Centres, please,” “When are centres?” “Help,” or “Break.”
  • Countdown and timer: Give advance notice of when centres will begin.
  • Environmental adjustments: Keep the teacher’s magnets out of reach and use a light-switch cover if necessary.
  • Modelling and practice: Read and role-play the story when Sam is calm, not only after challenging behaviour.
  • Positive reinforcement: Give immediate, specific feedback.
    “You checked your schedule and kept the magnets safe—great morning routine.”
  • Consistent staff response: All staff should use the same short prompts and avoid lengthy explanations during escalation.
  • Regulation support: Offer movement or sensory input before the morning instructional activity if Sam needs it.
  • Data collection: Record what happens before and after the behaviour to determine whether its purpose is gaining access, escaping or delaying work, seeking sensory input, or gaining adult attention.
