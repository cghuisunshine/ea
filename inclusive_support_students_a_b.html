Classroom Adaptations

Profile-based supports that preserve meaningful learning while improving access, participation, and success.

A

Terry, Grade 5

Student profile

Terry is in Grade 5 and receives special education services for reading for 30 minutes each day. He spends the remainder of the day in the regular classroom. He receives Grade 5 instruction in all core curriculum areas except reading. Decoding is difficult for him, but comprehension is not a significant problem.

Support focus: Because Terry understands what he reads once words are decoded, keep the Grade 5 learning outcomes the same and adapt how he accesses written information.

Provide audio or read-aloud support

A teacher, peer, or text-to-speech tool can read longer questions, instructions, and content aloud. His decoding difficulty will not prevent him from demonstrating comprehension.

Classroom Adaptations Article

Use differentiated or supported reading materials

When appropriate, provide more accessible text or enhance the original text by highlighting important information and adding pictures.

Brookes Blog

Use visuals and graphic organizers

Pictures, diagrams, maps, and organizers help Terry access information without relying entirely on decoding written text.

Brookes Blog

Allow alternative ways of getting information

For example, Terry can listen to a chapter while following along with the written text instead of decoding everything independently.

Support lengthy reading assignments

Break longer reading into smaller sections. This makes the task more manageable and allows Terry to experience success along the way.

Main goal for Terry: Do not make the academic content easier just because decoding is difficult; make the content more accessible.

B

Josh, Grade 8

Student profile

Josh is in Grade 8 and has a Category H designation: Severe Behaviour Disorder. His academics are well below grade level in all areas. He receives instruction at the Grade 5 level in reading and the Grade 6 level in math. His reading difficulties make the science and social studies curriculum difficult to access; however, he is successful when someone helps him read the content and when assignments and tests are given orally. He has difficulty staying on task and may become aggressive when frustrated.

Support focus: Josh needs more substantial adaptation because he is working below Grade 8 level academically, has difficulty maintaining attention, and becomes frustrated.

Use reading material at his instructional level

For science and social studies, provide simplified or differentiated text while still teaching the important concepts.

Brookes Blog

Continue oral assignments and testing

Josh succeeds when content is read to him and he can respond orally. He may give oral answers, presentations, charts, or other demonstrations instead of completing every assignment in writing.

Brookes Blog

Break assignments into manageable chunks

Give Josh one short section at a time instead of a long worksheet or assignment all at once. This reduces overwhelm and creates frequent opportunities for success.

Classroom Adaptations Article

Reduce the amount of work when appropriate

Josh might complete fewer questions that demonstrate the same skill rather than completing an entire worksheet.

Brookes Blog

Provide a low-distraction seating area

Seat Josh near the teacher and away from high-traffic or distracting areas to help him maintain attention.

Classroom Adaptations Article + Brookes Blog

Use a checklist and predictable routine

Provide a short visual sequence: 1. Read or listen to directions → 2. Complete three questions → 3. Check work → 4. Ask for help.

Classroom Adaptations Article

Increase assistance and immediate feedback

A teacher, peer, special educator, or educational assistant can model the first step, prompt Josh when he becomes off-task, and provide immediate feedback.

✓

Main goal for Josh: Match support to his instructional level, make tasks predictable, and reduce frustration so he can participate successfully.
